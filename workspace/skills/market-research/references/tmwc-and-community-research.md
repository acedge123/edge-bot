# TMWC and Community Research

Use this file when the study involves Mom Walk Collective / Mom Walk Connect style communities, IRL events, organizers, ambassadors, or creator/community-led insight capture.

## Why this is different

Community-powered research is not just cheaper panel replacement. It can produce richer contextual signal because it includes:

- longitudinal identity
- organizer relationships
- city-level nuance
- event participation behavior
- emotional and social context
- creator / ambassador influence

Treat these as differentiated assets, but do not overstate representativeness.

## Best-fit use cases

- understanding local mom needs or city-specific tensions
- testing concepts with trusted community members
- measuring IRL activation response
- comparing organizer perceptions with participant perceptions
- building longitudinal sentiment reads
- identifying language, routines, and barriers for moms

## Strong methods for this context

### Ambassador / organizer interviews
Best when the question involves community mechanics, recurring objections, trust, or activation quality.

### Community pulse surveys
Best for fast directional reads inside an existing engaged population.

### Mobile diary studies
Best for lived experience, routines, emotional states, and behavior over time.

### Walk / event intercepts
Best for immediate reaction to activations, local partnerships, and in-the-moment experience.

### City cohort studies
Best when geographic context matters and the user wants reusable cohort intelligence over time.

### Creator-led qualitative research
Best when trust and cultural fluency matter more than moderator neutrality.

## Common cautions

- community sample does not equal general-market sample
- organizer voices are useful but not identical to participant truth
- highly engaged members may skew more positive or more articulate than the base
- local activation results may not generalize nationally

## Good recommendation pattern

When community signal is rich but not representative, say something like:

> Use the community to generate and refine hypotheses quickly, then validate broader prevalence separately if the decision requires general-market confidence.

## Good output additions for this domain

Consider including:
- local vs national caveats
- organizer and participant split
- behavioral + attitudinal readout
- implications for programming, partnerships, creator strategy, or city rollout
